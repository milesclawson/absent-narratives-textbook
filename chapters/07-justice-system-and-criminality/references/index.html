    References: The Justice System and the Construction of Criminality¶

    1. Mass incarceration in the United States - Wikipedia - Traces the growth of the U.S. prison population from the 1970s "war on drugs" through mandatory minimums, with racial disparity statistics showing Black Americans imprisoned at five times the white rate. Anchors the chapter's central case study.

    2. Racial profiling - Wikipedia - Defines racial profiling and traces its roots to slave patrols, with international comparisons and documented cases. Directly supports the chapter's coverage of selective enforcement and policing history.

    3. School-to-prison pipeline - Wikipedia - Documents how zero-tolerance discipline policies funnel students, disproportionately Black students, out of school and into the justice system, with restorative justice offered as an alternative. Matches the chapter's juvenile justice discussion closely.

    4. The New Jim Crow: Mass Incarceration in the Age of Colorblindness - Michelle Alexander - The New Press - A landmark argument that mass incarceration functions as a racial caste system, providing the chapter's core theoretical framework for criminality as a social construction.

    5. Just Mercy: A Story of Justice and Redemption - Bryan Stevenson - Spiegel & Grau - A memoir by the founder of the Equal Justice Initiative documenting wrongful convictions and sentencing disparities, giving students a case-based complement to the chapter's statistical evidence.

    6. Racial Justice - Equal Justice Initiative - Connects the history of enslavement, lynching, and segregation directly to mass incarceration today, supporting the chapter's argument that current enforcement patterns have deep historical roots.

    7. Criminal Justice Fact Sheet - NAACP - Compiles current incarceration, arrest, and sentencing disparity statistics by race, including juvenile justice data, giving students concrete numbers for the chapter's sentencing disparity discussion.

    8. Mass Incarceration - ACLU - Describes the Campaign for Smart Justice's state-by-state effort to reduce incarceration and address racial disparities in sentencing and policing, supporting the chapter's discussion of policy responses.

    9. Restorative Justice: An Overview - Office of Justice Programs, U.S. Department of Justice - A federal overview of restorative justice principles as an alternative to punitive enforcement, directly supporting the chapter's restorative justice and due process discussion.

    10. Restorative Practices and School Discipline - California Department of Education - Explains how restorative practices reduce reliance on suspension and expulsion in schools, giving students a concrete policy alternative to the school-to-prison pipeline documented earlier in the chapter.
