    Quiz: Indigenous Sovereignty and Tribal Relations¶

    Test your understanding of Indigenous sovereignty, treaty rights, and tribal relations with these review questions.

    1. How does this chapter define Indigenous sovereignty?¶

    1. The inherent, pre-existing right of Indigenous nations to govern their own citizens, territory, laws, and cultural practices, a right that predates the United States and is only recognized, not granted, by it
    2. A right created by the U.S. Congress in the 1970s
    3. A status that applies only to nations that have signed a treaty in the last fifty years
    4. A ceremonial title with no legal significance
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. Indigenous sovereignty is the inherent, pre-existing right of Indigenous nations to govern themselves, predating the United States and only recognized or violated, never granted, by it — a distinction that determines whether tribal authority can be legitimately taken away by Congress. Option B reverses this origin, treating a pre-existing right as a federal creation. Option C invents a time restriction not in the text. Option D contradicts the chapter's emphasis on real legal rights, such as treaty rights.

    Concept Tested: Indigenous Sovereignty

    2. What was the stated policy goal of the boarding school era, as documented in period government records?¶

    1. Preparing Indigenous students for careers in tribal government
    2. Preserving and strengthening Indigenous languages and cultural traditions
    3. Cultural elimination through forced assimilation, achieved by separating children from their families, languages, and cultural practices
    4. Providing bilingual education in both English and Indigenous languages
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The chapter states plainly that the boarding school era's explicit, documented policy goal was cultural elimination, not education in the ordinary sense, achieved by forcibly separating Indigenous children from their families, languages, and cultural practices. Option A and D both invent goals consistent with genuine education, which the chapter explicitly denies was the purpose. Option B directly contradicts the era's documented assimilationist intent.

    Concept Tested: Boarding School Era

    3. Why does the chapter describe federal recognition as "a legal gate"?¶

    1. Because federal recognition determines whether a nation's sovereignty exists at all as a people
    2. Because nations without federal recognition face significant additional barriers to exercising sovereignty, even though their sovereignty as a people does not depend on federal permission in the first place
    3. Because federal recognition was eliminated by the self-determination policy of the 1970s
    4. Because federal recognition applies only to nations located outside of Minnesota
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. Federal recognition is described as a legal gate because nations without it face significant additional barriers to exercising sovereignty and accessing federal services, even though the chapter is explicit that a people's sovereignty does not depend on federal permission to exist. Option A contradicts the chapter's core distinction between inherent sovereignty and its federal acknowledgment. Option C misstates the relationship between two distinct concepts. Option D invents a geographic restriction not in the text.

    Concept Tested: Federal Recognition

    4. What does the chapter say about the origin of blood quantum as a concept?¶

    1. Blood quantum was invented by Indigenous nations themselves as their own enrollment standard
    2. Blood quantum is a modern concept created after the self-determination policy era began
    3. Blood quantum applies only to nations that have not achieved federal recognition
    4. Blood quantum is a federally imposed measure historically used to determine tribal enrollment eligibility and gradually reduce the population legally counted as Indigenous, and many tribal nations today set their own enrollment criteria independent of it
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. The chapter is explicit that blood quantum is a federal administrative tool, not a concept Indigenous nations invented for themselves, and that many tribal nations today exercise self-determination by setting their own enrollment criteria independent of it. Option A directly contradicts this federal origin. Option B misplaces its historical timing, since blood quantum predates the self-determination era. Option C invents a restriction not found in the text.

    Concept Tested: Blood Quantum

    5. Why can a 19th-century treaty still determine 21st-century fishing regulations in parts of Minnesota, according to this chapter?¶

    1. Because treaty rights remain legally binding today regardless of how long ago they were signed or how often they have been violated
    2. Because all 19th-century treaties automatically expire and are renegotiated every fifty years
    3. Because fishing regulations are set entirely independent of any treaty or legal agreement
    4. Because Minnesota state law overrides all federally recognized treaty rights
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. Treaty rights — to land, hunting and fishing, resource management, and self-governance — remain legally binding today regardless of their age or history of violation, which is why a 19th-century agreement can still shape 21st-century regulations. Option B invents an expiration mechanism the chapter denies exists. Option C contradicts the chapter's entire point about treaties' ongoing legal force. Option D reverses the actual legal relationship the chapter describes.

    Concept Tested: Treaty Rights

    6. A state agency begins co-managing natural resources jointly with a tribal government, sharing real decision-making authority rather than issuing a symbolic statement. Applying the chapter's evaluative spectrum, which concept does this action move toward?¶

    1. Blood quantum, because it involves a federal administrative measure
    2. Indigenous iconography, because it involves visual representation
    3. Self-determination, since it represents tribal nations exercising governing authority over resource management with reduced federal or state control, rather than a purely symbolic gesture
    4. Cultural suppression, because it involves restricting cultural practices
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. Self-determination is the principle that Indigenous nations have the right to govern their own affairs, including resource management, and joint resource co-management with real shared authority represents movement toward that material, structural end of the chapter's symbolic-to-material spectrum. Option A misapplies a concept about ancestry measurement. Option B misapplies a concept about symbols and imagery. Option D describes the opposite of what this scenario shows.

    Concept Tested: Self-Determination

    7. A university returns ancestral remains and sacred objects, originally taken without consent, to the tribal nation they came from, following a federal law's requirements. Which concept does this scenario apply?¶

    1. Federal recognition, because it involves a formal government process
    2. Repatriation, the return of Indigenous cultural items, ancestral remains, and sacred objects to their originating tribal nations, partially mandated by federal law
    3. Land acknowledgment, because it involves recognizing original inhabitants of land
    4. Sovereignty erosion, because it involves a federal legal process
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. This scenario directly matches the chapter's definition of repatriation: the return of cultural items, remains, and sacred objects to their originating tribal nations, a process now partially mandated by NAGPRA, the Native American Graves Protection and Repatriation Act. Option A misapplies a concept about acknowledging sovereign status generally. Option C misapplies a concept about a spoken statement rather than physical return. Option D misapplies a concept describing the opposite, ongoing direction of change.

    Concept Tested: Repatriation

    8. A sports team uses a Native-themed mascot that Indigenous organizations have identified as disrespectful and inaccurate. Which concept from this chapter does this scenario illustrate?¶

    1. Contemporary Indigenous voice, because Indigenous people are directly represented
    2. Treaty rights, because it involves resource management
    3. Federal recognition, because it involves government acknowledgment
    4. Indigenous iconography, symbols and imagery associated with Indigenous cultures that have been appropriated by non-Indigenous institutions such as sports mascots in ways many Indigenous communities have identified as disrespectful or inaccurate
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. This scenario matches the chapter's discussion of Indigenous iconography as a contested site, frequently appropriated by non-Indigenous institutions like sports mascots and brand logos, connecting directly to Chapter 8's discussion of stereotype in media. Option A misapplies a concept about Indigenous people speaking for themselves, the opposite of appropriation. Option B and C both misapply concepts about legal and governmental processes unrelated to symbolic representation.

    Concept Tested: Indigenous Iconography

    9. Why does the chapter emphasize that the distinction between Indigenous sovereignty being "granted" versus "recognized" by the United States "is not a technicality"?¶

    1. Because the distinction determines whether tribal governing authority can be legitimately taken away by an act of Congress, or is instead a standing right federal policy is obligated to respect
    2. Because both words mean exactly the same thing in federal Indian law
    3. Because the distinction only matters for nations located outside Minnesota
    4. Because "recognized" sovereignty automatically expires after federal recognition is granted
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. The chapter argues this distinction carries real legal weight: if sovereignty were merely granted by the U.S. government, it could in principle be legitimately revoked by Congress, whereas recognizing a pre-existing right obligates federal policy to respect it as standing, not conditional. Option B denies the meaningful legal difference the chapter establishes. Option C invents a geographic limitation. Option D confuses recognition with an expiration mechanism not described.

    Concept Tested: Indigenous Sovereignty

    10. How does the chapter connect contemporary Indigenous voice to the erased Indigenous voices discussed in Chapter 4 and the misappropriated Indigenous iconography discussed earlier in this chapter?¶

    1. Contemporary Indigenous voice has no meaningful connection to either concept
    2. Contemporary Indigenous voice describes historical records only, not present-day speakers
    3. Contemporary Indigenous voice refers to present-day Indigenous authors, journalists, artists, and leaders speaking for themselves, serving as the direct, ongoing corrective to both the erasure Chapter 4 documented and the misappropriated iconography this chapter describes
    4. Contemporary Indigenous voice is identical in meaning to blood quantum
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The chapter explicitly positions contemporary Indigenous voice — present-day Indigenous authors, journalists, artists, and leaders speaking for themselves — as the direct, ongoing corrective to both Chapter 4's erased Indigenous voices and this chapter's discussion of misappropriated Indigenous iconography. Option A denies this explicit connection. Option B contradicts the concept's present-day focus. Option D conflates two entirely unrelated concepts.

    Concept Tested: Contemporary Indigenous Voice
