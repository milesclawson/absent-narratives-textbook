# References: Foundations of Historical Inquiry

1. [Historiography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historiography) - Wikipedia - Traces how the writing of history has changed across cultures and eras, covering major schools of thought. Grounds this chapter's claim that history is written, not simply discovered.

2. [Primary source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_source) - Wikipedia - Explains the distinction between primary and secondary sources, including how the same document can shift categories depending on the question being asked. Directly supports the chapter's source-comparison table.

3. [Historical method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_method) - Wikipedia - Details the techniques historians use to evaluate sources, including the six questions of source criticism and the logic of corroboration. Expands on the chapter's five-question source-evaluation framework.

4. The Landscape of History: How Historians Map the Past - John Lewis Gaddis - Oxford University Press - A working historian's accessible explanation of historical method, causation, and interpretation, useful for students building the analytical toolkit this chapter introduces.

5. A People's History of the United States - Howard Zinn - Harper Perennial - A landmark counter-narrative history that foregrounds voices excluded from traditional textbooks, offering students a concrete model of what recovering an absent narrative looks like in practice.

6. [Analyzing Primary Sources](https://www.archives.gov/education/lessons/worksheets) - National Archives - Downloadable worksheets and a four-step process for analyzing photographs, documents, and artifacts, giving students a practical version of the source-questioning skills this chapter teaches.

7. [Primary Source Analysis Tool](https://www.loc.gov/teachers/primary-source-analysis-tool) - Library of Congress - A graphic-organizer tool and format-specific teacher guides for observing, reflecting on, and questioning primary sources, reinforcing this chapter's emphasis on questioning rather than absorbing sources.

8. [Our Mission & Vision](https://www.facinghistory.org/about) - Facing History & Ourselves - Describes a nonprofit's approach to using historical case studies to build critical thinking and empathy, modeling this chapter's pairing of analytical rigor with perspective-taking.

9. [Thinking Like a Historian](https://www.khanacademy.org/humanities/us-history/precontact-and-early-colonial-era/how-to-think-like-a-historian/v/thinking-like-a-historian) - Khan Academy - A short video introducing sourcing, contextualization, and corroboration as historical thinking skills, reinforcing the chapter's cross-checking process.

10. [Why Study History?](https://www.historians.org/why-history-matters/why-study-history/) - American Historical Association - The professional association of historians explains why historical thinking builds empathy and critical judgment, supporting the chapter's closing argument that evidence and empathy must work together.
