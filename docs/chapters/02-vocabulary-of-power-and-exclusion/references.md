# References: The Vocabulary of Power and Exclusion

1. [Privilege (social inequality)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privilege_(social_inequality)) - Wikipedia - Traces privilege theory from W.E.B. Du Bois through Peggy McIntosh's influential essay, covering racial, gender, and class privilege. Provides historical grounding for this chapter's core vocabulary term.

2. [Stereotype](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stereotype) - Wikipedia - Explains how stereotypes form, spread, and produce effects like stereotype threat and self-fulfilling prophecy. Supports the chapter's distinction between stereotype, prejudice, and discrimination.

3. [Discrimination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrimination) - Wikipedia - Surveys discrimination across race, gender, disability, and other categories, distinguishing individual acts from institutional and systemic patterns. Anchors the chapter's move from attitude to action.

4. Why Are All the Black Kids Sitting Together in the Cafeteria? - Beverly Daniel Tatum - Basic Books - A psychologist's accessible explanation of racial identity development, stereotype, and systemic bias, giving students a framework for the chapter's vocabulary beyond dictionary definitions.

5. So You Want to Talk About Race - Ijeoma Oluo - Seal Press - A widely used trade book that defines and illustrates privilege, tokenism, and systemic oppression through concrete contemporary examples, complementing the chapter's abstract definitions.

6. [Systemic Equality: Addressing America's Legacy of Racism and Systemic Discrimination](https://www.aclu.org/news/topic/systemic-equality-addressing-americas-legacy-of-racism-and-systemic-discrimination) - ACLU - Explains how systemic racism operates through institutions and policy rather than individual attitudes alone, illustrating this chapter's distinction between bias and systemic oppression.

7. [Deep Divisions in Americans' Views of Nation's Racial History](https://www.pewresearch.org/politics/2021/08/12/deep-divisions-in-americans-views-of-nations-racial-history-and-how-to-address-it/) - Pew Research Center - National survey data on how Americans perceive racial inequality and its causes, giving students current evidence to weigh against the chapter's definitions of systemic oppression and power structure.

8. [Social Justice Standards](https://www.learningforjustice.org/frameworks/social-justice-standards) - Learning for Justice (Southern Poverty Law Center) - An anti-bias framework defining identity, diversity, justice, and action for classroom use, directly modeling this chapter's vocabulary of prejudice, discrimination, and inclusion.

9. [Equity vs Equality in Education](https://www.unesco.org/en/query-list/e/equity-vs-equality-education) - UNESCO - The UN's education agency distinguishes equity from equality with concrete examples, clarifying the chapter's often-confused pair of terms.

10. [Racism, Bias, and Discrimination](https://www.apa.org/topics/racism-bias-discrimination) - American Psychological Association - The APA's psychological definitions of prejudice, bias, and discrimination at both individual and institutional levels, giving precise disciplinary definitions for the chapter's key terms.
