# References: Case Study: Manifest Destiny

1. [Manifest destiny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manifest_destiny) - Wikipedia - Explains the ideology of American exceptionalism and divine providence behind westward expansion, including its consequences for Indigenous nations and internal opposition at the time. Provides the chapter's central case study in one article.

2. [Trail of Tears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trail_of_Tears) - Wikipedia - Documents the forced removal of the "Five Civilized Tribes," including tribe-by-tribe casualty data, the Indian Removal Act, and Supreme Court cases like Worcester v. Georgia. Supplies the chapter's central example of Indigenous displacement.

3. [Homestead Acts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homestead_Acts) - Wikipedia - Explains how the Homestead Acts distributed land taken from Native nations to settlers, including the later Dawes Act's reduction of reservation land from 138 million to 48 million acres. Connects land dispossession directly to federal policy.

4. Empire of the Summer Moon - S.C. Gwynne - Scribner - A narrative history of the Comanche and westward expansion that complicates the simple frontier story, giving students an example of a fuller account than the standard textbook narrative.

5. An Indigenous Peoples' History of the United States - Roxanne Dunbar-Ortiz - Beacon Press - A widely assigned counter-narrative history centering Indigenous perspectives on settler colonialism, directly modeling the chapter's assignment to recover erased Indigenous voices.

6. [Jackson's Message to Congress on Indian Removal (1830)](https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/jacksons-message-to-congress-on-indian-removal) - National Archives - The primary source document that set federal removal policy in motion, with historical context and a full transcript, letting students read the dominant narrative's own justification firsthand.

7. [The Homestead Act of 1862](https://www.archives.gov/education/lessons/homestead-act) - National Archives - A teaching guide and primary source lesson on the Homestead Act, useful for pairing settlers' land claims against the dispossession documented elsewhere in this chapter.

8. [Trail of Tears National Historic Trail](https://www.nps.gov/trte/) - National Park Service - The official site of the multi-state historic trail administered in partnership with Cherokee Nation, the United Keetoowah Band, and the Eastern Band of Cherokee Indians, who designated it a Sacred Site in 2016.

9. [American Indian Removal: What Does It Mean to Remove a People?](https://americanindian.si.edu/nk360/removal/) - Smithsonian National Museum of the American Indian, Native Knowledge 360° - A classroom resource built from Indigenous perspectives explaining how removal was justified and resisted, directly supplying the erased Indigenous voices this chapter asks students to recover.

10. [Manifest Destiny and the West](https://www.nga.gov/learn/teachers/lessons-activities/uncovering-america/manifest-destiny-west.html) - National Gallery of Art - Analyzes how 19th-century paintings visually constructed the frontier myth and American exceptionalism, showing students how the dominant narrative was built and circulated through art.
