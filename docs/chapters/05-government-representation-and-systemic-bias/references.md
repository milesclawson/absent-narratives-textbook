# References: Government Representation and Systemic Bias

1. [Gerrymandering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerrymandering) - Wikipedia - Traces the practice from its 1812 Massachusetts origin through modern "cracking" and "packing" techniques and current reform proposals. Supports the chapter's treatment of gerrymandering and redistricting as tools of systemic bias.

2. [Voting Rights Act of 1965](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voting_Rights_Act_of_1965) - Wikipedia - Details the Act's core provisions, its legislative history, and the Supreme Court decisions like Shelby County v. Holder that later weakened it. Anchors the chapter's discussion of the law meant to curtail voter suppression.

3. [Voter suppression in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voter_suppression_in_the_United_States) - Wikipedia - Documents poll taxes, literacy tests, and modern suppression tactics with specific historical cases and state-by-state examples. Directly supports the chapter's core vocabulary of exclusionary voting practices.

4. One Person, No Vote: How Voter Suppression Is Destroying Our Democracy - Carol Anderson - Bloomsbury Publishing - A National Book Award finalist tracing voter suppression from Reconstruction to the present, giving students a sustained historical argument to pair with the chapter's civics vocabulary.

5. Give Us the Ballot: The Modern Struggle for Voting Rights in America - Ari Berman - Farrar, Straus and Giroux - A history of the Voting Rights Act's passage and erosion, providing depth on the legislative battles the chapter introduces in survey form.

6. [Fighting Voter Suppression](https://www.aclu.org/issues/voting-rights/fighting-voter-suppression) - ACLU - Describes current voter suppression tactics such as voter ID laws, roll purges, and cuts to early voting, and the ACLU's litigation against them, updating the chapter's historical examples with contemporary cases.

7. [Congress and the Voting Rights Act of 1965](https://www.archives.gov/legislative/features/voting-rights-1965) - National Archives - Covers the legislative history and immediate impact of the Voting Rights Act, including registration data from 1965-1967, supplementing the chapter's account of the Act's passage.

8. [Tribal Nations & the United States: An Introduction](https://www.ncai.org/about-tribes) - National Congress of American Indians - Explains tribal sovereignty and the nation-to-nation relationship between tribal governments and the federal government, directly supporting the chapter's treatment of a category of representation civics courses often omit.

9. [Most Americans Don't Feel Strongly About Redistricting](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2022/03/04/with-legislative-redistricting-at-a-crucial-stage-most-americans-dont-feel-strongly-about-it/) - Pew Research Center - Survey data showing most Americans oppose partisan gerrymandering while few understand redistricting processes, giving students current public-opinion evidence for the chapter's discussion of civic participation and trust.

10. [Laws and Court Cases](https://www.archives.gov/research/african-americans/vote/laws-and-courts) - National Archives - A curated overview of the legal history of Black voting rights from Reconstruction through the Voting Rights Act, connecting the chapter's poll tax and literacy test examples to their eventual legal reversal.
