#### 1. How does this chapter define redlining?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. A government-imposed ban on trade with a specific country
2. The practice of giving preferential college admission to applicants whose relatives attended the same institution
3. A discriminatory practice, formalized by federal housing agencies beginning in the 1930s, of denying mortgages or insurance based on a neighborhood's racial or ethnic composition, regardless of an individual applicant's creditworthiness
4. The unfair treatment of individuals seeking to rent housing based on gender
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. Redlining was a discriminatory federal housing practice, formalized in the 1930s, that denied mortgages or insurance to entire neighborhoods based on racial or ethnic composition rather than individual creditworthiness, and the chapter calls it the clearest documented example of systemic racism translated directly into government maps. Option A describes an embargo. Option B describes legacy admissions. Option D describes housing discrimination more broadly, the wider category redlining exemplifies.

    **Concept Tested:** Redlining

#### 3. Why does the chapter say the wealth gap is significantly larger than the income gap between the same demographic groups?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Because wealth and income are defined as the exact same measurement
2. Because income is always higher than wealth for every family regardless of history
3. Because the wealth gap only measures differences in annual salary
4. Because wealth compounds across generations while income resets each year, so two families with identical current incomes can have dramatically different wealth depending on past access to homeownership
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **D**. The chapter distinguishes wealth, which compounds across generations through assets like home equity, from income, which resets each year — meaning two families with identical current incomes can hold very different wealth depending on whether earlier generations could buy a home. Option A collapses a distinction the chapter draws carefully. Option B makes an unsupported blanket claim. Option C misdefines wealth gap as an income measurement.

    **Concept Tested:** Wealth Gap

#### 5. What "genuine analytical puzzle" does the chapter identify in gentrification?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>

</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. Option A denies gentrification's defining feature. Option B contradicts the chapter's account of gentrification following disinvestment. Option D collapses two distinct, sequential concepts into one.

</div>

    The correct answer is **B**. Option A denies the very mechanism the chapter documents. Option C ignores the multi-decade gap the chapter describes between these events. Option D wrongly narrows disinvestment's scope, contradicting the chapter's list of infrastructure, business lending, and schools.
