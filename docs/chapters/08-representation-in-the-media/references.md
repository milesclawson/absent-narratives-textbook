# References: Representation in the Media

1. [Representation of African Americans in media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representation_of_African_Americans_in_media) - Wikipedia - Catalogs historical stereotype archetypes and documents occupational and ownership disparities across film, television, and news. Provides a detailed case study of the chapter's media representation concept.

2. [Whitewashing in film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitewashing_in_film) - Wikipedia - Traces whitewashed casting from early blackface and yellowface through contemporary controversies like #StarringJohnCho, including studios' own financial justifications. Directly supports the chapter's whitewashing and tokenized casting concepts.

3. [Algorithmic bias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algorithmic_bias) - Wikipedia - Explains how biased training data and design choices produce unfair automated outcomes, with real-world cases in hiring, criminal justice, and facial recognition. Grounds the chapter's digital and algorithmic media bias discussion.

4. Covering: The Hidden Assault on Our Civil Rights - Kenji Yoshino - Random House - Examines how mainstream visibility often requires marginalized people to downplay identity, connecting directly to the chapter's discussion of tokenized casting and stereotype in media.

5. Amusing Ourselves to Death: Public Discourse in the Age of Show Business - Neil Postman - Penguin Books - A classic media studies argument about how television's form shapes what counts as serious public discourse, giving students a theoretical lens for the chapter's framing effect and headline bias discussion.

6. [Framing the News](https://www.pewresearch.org/journalism/1998/07/13/framing-the-news/) - Pew Research Center - A foundational study of how journalists use narrative frames rather than straight reporting, showing how word choice shapes audience interpretation of the same event. Supports the chapter's framing effect and headline bias concepts.

7. [Lesson Plan: What's in the Frame?](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/classroom/lesson-plans/2022/06/lesson-plan-whats-in-the-frame-how-media-literacy-builds-students-understanding-of-the-news) - PBS NewsHour Classroom - A classroom-tested lesson teaching students how editorial framing choices shape meaning, directly practicing the chapter's media literacy skill in an interactive format.

8. [Be MediaWise](https://www.pbslearningmedia.org/collection/be-mediawise-news-literacy-lesson-plans/) - PBS LearningMedia, developed with the Poynter Institute - A media literacy lesson collection teaching students to fact-check, spot propaganda, and detect misinformation online, supporting the chapter's propaganda and counter-storytelling concepts.

9. [Disability Language Style Guide](https://ncdj.org/style-guide/) - National Center on Disability and Journalism, Arizona State University - A journalism style guide explaining how word choice shapes disability stereotypes in reporting, extending the chapter's stereotype-in-media discussion to a group beyond race.

10. [GLAAD Media Reference Guide](https://glaad.org/reference/) - GLAAD - An industry-standard guide, used by the AP and major newsrooms, for accurate and non-stereotypical LGBTQ media coverage, broadening the chapter's representation and stereotype concepts to another marginalized group.
