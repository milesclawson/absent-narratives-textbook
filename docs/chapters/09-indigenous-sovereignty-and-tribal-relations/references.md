# References: Indigenous Sovereignty and Tribal Relations

1. [Tribal sovereignty in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribal_sovereignty_in_the_United_States) - Wikipedia - Explains the constitutional basis for tribal sovereignty, treaty rights cases like the Boldt Decision, and the limits of tribal court jurisdiction. Provides the legal foundation for this chapter's core concept.

2. [American Indian boarding schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Indian_boarding_schools) - Wikipedia - Documents forced assimilation practices, elevated child mortality rates, and Indigenous resistance across centuries of federal boarding school policy. Anchors the chapter's boarding school era and cultural suppression sections.

3. [Native American Graves Protection and Repatriation Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_American_Graves_Protection_and_Repatriation_Act) - Wikipedia - Explains NAGPRA's repatriation requirements, the activism of Maria Pearson, and unresolved cases like Kennewick Man. Directly supports the chapter's repatriation concept.

4. Custer Died for Your Sins: An Indian Manifesto - Vine Deloria Jr. - University of Oklahoma Press - A foundational work of Indigenous political thought arguing for tribal self-determination, giving students a primary theoretical voice behind the chapter's sovereignty and self-determination concepts.

5. The Rediscovery of America: Native Peoples and the Unmaking of U.S. History - Ned Blackhawk - Yale University Press - A National Book Award-winning history centering Native nations as political actors across U.S. history, modeling the contemporary Indigenous voice this chapter asks students to recover.

6. [Tribal Nations in Minnesota](https://mn.gov/indian-affairs/tribal-nations-in-minnesota/) - Minnesota Indian Affairs Council - The state's official listing and description of Minnesota's 11 sovereign tribal nations, seven Anishinaabe reservations and four Dakota communities, fulfilling this chapter's Minnesota Tribal Nations standard directly.

7. [Federal Indian Boarding School Initiative](https://www.doi.gov/priorities/strengthening-indian-country/federal-indian-boarding-school-initiative) - U.S. Department of the Interior - The federal government's own investigative report documenting 408 boarding schools, burial sites, and appropriations from 1819-1969, giving students primary federal acknowledgment of the boarding school era.

8. [Boarding Schools](https://americanindian.si.edu/online-resources/topic-listing?topic=22) - Smithsonian National Museum of the American Indian, Native Knowledge 360° - Classroom-ready resources built with Native educators addressing boarding schools, cultural repression, and language revitalization, supporting the chapter's cultural suppression and self-determination discussion.

9. [Native American Graves Protection and Repatriation Act](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/nagpra/) - National Park Service - The federal agency's operational guidance on repatriating human remains and sacred objects to tribal nations, supplementing the chapter's repatriation section with current implementation detail.

10. [Tribal Nations & the United States: An Introduction](https://www.ncai.org/about-tribes) - National Congress of American Indians - Explains treaty rights, federal recognition, and the nation-to-nation relationship from a tribal advocacy perspective, extending the government-to-government framework introduced in Chapter 5 into this chapter's deeper treatment.
