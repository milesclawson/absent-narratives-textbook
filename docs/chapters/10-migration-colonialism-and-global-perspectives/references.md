# References: Migration, Colonialism, and Global Perspectives

1. [Colonialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonialism) - Wikipedia - Distinguishes settler, exploitation, surrogate, and internal colonialism across European empires, with sections on socioeconomic impact and decolonization. Provides the chapter's core typology for analyzing colonial power.

2. [Forced migration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forced_migration) - Wikipedia - Surveys displacement caused by conflict, disaster, and persecution worldwide, citing an estimated 100 million forcibly displaced people by the end of 2022 and the 1951 Refugee Convention framework. Anchors the chapter's global migration pattern concept.

3. [Diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diaspora) - Wikipedia - Traces the concept from the Jewish diaspora through Irish, Chinese, and contemporary Syrian and Venezuelan examples, with scholarly frameworks for analyzing dispersed communities. Directly supports the chapter's diaspora concept.

4. Guns, Germs, and Steel: The Fates of Human Societies - Jared Diamond - W.W. Norton & Company - A widely read account of how geography and conquest shaped global power imbalances, giving students a macro-historical frame for the chapter's colonialism and imperialism concepts.

5. Exit West - Mohsin Hamid - Riverhead Books - A widely taught novel following refugees through magical-realist doors between countries, giving students a literary entry point into the chapter's forced migration and refugee experience concepts.

6. [Global Trends Report](https://www.unhcr.org/global-trends) - UNHCR, the UN Refugee Agency - The UN's annual official statistics on refugees, asylum-seekers, and internally displaced people worldwide, giving students current data for the chapter's global migration pattern and refugee experience discussion.

7. [How U.S. Immigration Laws and Rules Have Changed Through History](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2015/09/30/how-u-s-immigration-laws-and-rules-have-changed-through-history/) - Pew Research Center - Traces U.S. immigration policy from the 1790 Naturalization Act through 1965 reform, directly supporting the chapter's immigration policy and border formation discussion.

8. [Milestones in the History of U.S. Foreign Relations](https://history.state.gov/milestones) - U.S. Department of State, Office of the Historian - Short scholarly essays on major moments in American diplomatic history, including territorial expansion and overseas empire, supporting the chapter's U.S. foreign policy concept.

9. [Key Findings About U.S. Immigrants](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/08/21/key-findings-about-us-immigrants/) - Pew Research Center - Current demographic data on the U.S. immigrant population, complementing the chapter's historical immigration policy discussion with present-day figures.

10. [1898: Birth of an Overseas Empire](https://history.house.gov/Exhibitions-and-Publications/APA/Historical-Essays/Exclusion-and-Empire/1898/) - U.S. House of Representatives, Office of the Historian - Examines how the Spanish-American War turned the United States into an imperial power over the Philippines, Puerto Rico, and Guam, giving students a concrete case of American imperialism to pair with the chapter's global colonialism cases.
