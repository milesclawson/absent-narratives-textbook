# References: Civil Rights and Liberation Movements

1. [Civil rights movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_rights_movement) - Wikipedia - Surveys nonviolent strategies, key campaigns, and legislative outcomes from Montgomery through the Voting Rights Act. Provides the chapter's central narrative spine connecting strategy to legislative change.

2. [Jim Crow laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Crow_laws) - Wikipedia - Traces segregation from Plessy v. Ferguson through Brown v. Board of Education and its ongoing legacy, including felony disenfranchisement. Directly supports the chapter's Jim Crow era and desegregation sections.

3. [Reconstruction era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reconstruction_era) - Wikipedia - Covers the constitutional amendments, Freedmen's Bureau, and violent backlash of 1865-1877, with attention to competing historiographical interpretations. Anchors the chapter's legacy of slavery and Reconstruction era discussion.

4. Parting the Waters: America in the King Years, 1954-63 - Taylor Branch - Simon & Schuster - A Pulitzer Prize-winning narrative history of the civil rights movement's strategy and organizing, giving students a detailed model of the social movement strategy this chapter surveys.

5. Stonewall: The Riots That Sparked the Gay Revolution - David Carter - St. Martin's Press - The definitive history of the 1969 Stonewall uprising and the LGBTQ rights movement it launched, supporting the chapter's treatment of LGBTQ liberation as part of the same organizing tradition.

6. [Woman Suffrage and the 19th Amendment](https://www.archives.gov/education/lessons/woman-suffrage) - National Archives - A teaching resource built around the ratified amendment and suffrage petitions, supporting the chapter's suffrage movement discussion with primary source material.

7. [Stonewall National Monument: Rising for Equality](https://www.nps.gov/articles/stonewall-national-monument-rising-for-equality.htm) - National Park Service - Covers the 1969 Stonewall uprising at the first LGBTQ national monument, connecting a specific historic site to the chapter's LGBTQ rights movement discussion.

8. [Sitting-in for Disability Rights: The Section 504 Protests of the 1970s](https://www.americanhistory.si.edu/explore/stories/sitting-disability-rights-section-504-protests-1970s) - Smithsonian National Museum of American History - Documents the 1977 sit-in that forced enforcement of the first federal disability civil rights law, including cross-movement solidarity from Black Panther and labor groups, directly supporting the chapter's disability rights movement section.

9. [Voices of Civil Rights](https://guides.loc.gov/civil-rights-in-america) - Library of Congress - A resource guide compiling oral histories, photographs, and documents from movement participants, supporting the chapter's grassroots organizing and civil disobedience discussion with firsthand accounts.

10. [Travel Places of the Civil Rights Movement](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/travelweshallovercome/index.htm) - National Park Service - An itinerary of historic sites nationwide connected to the civil rights struggle, illustrating how the movement spanned far beyond its most famous events and locations.
