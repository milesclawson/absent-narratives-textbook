# References: Religion and Cultural Diffusion

1. [Religious persecution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_persecution) - Wikipedia - Surveys persecution across traditions from antiquity to the present, with a sociological framework distinguishing persecution from discrimination and intolerance. Anchors the chapter's religious persecution concept with historical breadth.

2. [Religious pluralism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_pluralism) - Wikipedia - Distinguishes tolerance, inclusivism, and ecumenism as different responses to religious diversity, with coverage of how Christianity, Buddhism, Islam, and other traditions have approached pluralism. Directly supports the chapter's religious pluralism concept.

3. [African diasporic religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_diasporic_religion) - Wikipedia - Documents how traditions like Candomblé, Vodun, and Santería developed across the Caribbean, Latin America, and the American South from West African roots. Supports the chapter's coverage of African diasporic religion as a product of forced migration and cultural diffusion.

4. The World's Religions - Huston Smith - HarperOne - A classic comparative-religion survey covering Hinduism, Buddhism, Judaism, Christianity, Islam, and Indigenous traditions from a historical and academic rather than devotional perspective, matching this chapter's non-theological framing.

5. A History of God: The 4,000-Year Quest of Judaism, Christianity, and Islam - Karen Armstrong - Ballantine Books - A historian's comparative account of how the Abrahamic traditions developed and diverged over four millennia, supporting the chapter's emphasis on religions as historically shaped rather than fixed.

6. [Religion and Public Life](https://rpl.hds.harvard.edu/what-we-do/our-approach/what-religious-literacy) - Harvard Divinity School - A non-devotional academic resource built for public school teachers that studies how religious traditions are internally diverse and change over time, directly modeling this chapter's approach to religion as history rather than theology.

7. [The Global Religious Landscape](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2012/12/18/global-religious-landscape-exec/) - Pew Research Center - A demographic study of the size and geographic distribution of the world's major religious groups, giving students current data to accompany the chapter's historical survey of religious traditions.

8. [Shamanism](https://www.britannica.com/topic/shamanism/Worldview) - Encyclopaedia Britannica - An academic overview of shamanic worldview and practice among northern Asian and other traditional societies, supporting the chapter's coverage of Shamanism and Animism as historically documented belief systems.

9. [Overview: Hinduism and Buddhism](https://www.khanacademy.org/humanities/world-history/x66f79d8a:empires-and-belief-systems/x66f79d8a:hinduism-and-buddhism-4-4/a/overview-hinduism-and-buddhism) - Khan Academy - A world history article tracing the shared origins and historical divergence of Hinduism and Buddhism in South Asia, supporting the chapter's cultural diffusion framework applied to these two traditions.

10. [Mission & History](https://pluralism.org/mission-and-history) - The Pluralism Project, Harvard University - Founded in 1991, this academic project studies America's changing religious landscape and interfaith relations, supporting the chapter's closing discussion of religious pluralism in a diverse society.
