# References: Research Methods and the Capstone Project

1. [Oral history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oral_history) - Wikipedia - Explains interviewing methodology, informed consent, and institutional standards across disciplines that use oral history as evidence. Directly supports the chapter's oral history method and interview technique concepts.

2. [Public history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_history) - Wikipedia - Traces the field's emergence from 1970s academic and community movements into museums, archives, and government work outside traditional classrooms. Anchors the chapter's public history and exhibit design concepts.

3. [Archival research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archival_research) - Wikipedia - Details how researchers locate collections, use finding aids, and navigate practical obstacles like handwriting and administrative jargon. Supports the chapter's archival research method directly.

4. Doing Oral History - Donald A. Ritchie - Oxford University Press - The standard practical manual for planning, conducting, and preserving oral history interviews, giving students a step-by-step guide for the capstone's interview technique component.

5. The Craft of Research - Wayne C. Booth, Gregory G. Colomb, and Joseph M. Williams - University of Chicago Press - A widely used guide to forming a research question, evaluating sources, and building an evidence-based argument, supporting the chapter's research question and source triangulation concepts.

6. [Oral History Best Practices](https://oralhistory.org/best-practices/) - Oral History Association - The professional organization's principles for ethical, collaborative interviewing, including informed consent and respect for narrators' communities. Supports the chapter's ethics of representation and interview technique concepts.

7. [Getting Started Overview](https://www.archives.gov/research/start) - National Archives - A practical guide to locating and using federal records for original research, giving students a concrete starting point for the capstone's archival research component.

8. [Statement on Standards of Professional Conduct](https://www.historians.org/resource/statement-on-standards-of-professional-conduct/) - American Historical Association - The historical profession's own ethical guidelines for research, interpretation, and honest communication of findings, directly supporting the chapter's ethics of representation and peer review discussion.

9. [What Is Public History?](https://ncph.org/) - National Council on Public History - Explains how historians create exhibits, documentaries, and community projects outside academic settings, supporting the chapter's historical documentary, exhibit design, and community action capstone options.

10. [Chicago Manual of Style Guide](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/research_and_citation/chicago_manual_18th_edition/cmos_formatting_and_style_guide/chicago_manual_of_style_18th_edition.html) - Purdue Online Writing Lab - A widely used citation reference covering both note-bibliography and author-date systems, giving students a practical tool for the chapter's citation practice requirement.
