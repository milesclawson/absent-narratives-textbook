# Chapters

This textbook is organized into 14 chapters covering 200 concepts.

## Chapter Overview

1. [Foundations of Historical Inquiry](01-foundations-of-historical-inquiry/index.md) - Introduces historiography, primary/secondary sources, bias, and the analytical vocabulary — narrative, counter-narrative, marginalization — that every later chapter depends on.
2. [The Vocabulary of Power and Exclusion](02-vocabulary-of-power-and-exclusion/index.md) - Builds the shared terminology of oppression, privilege, discrimination, and representation used across every subject-matter unit that follows.
3. [Identity, Race, and Social Construction](03-identity-race-and-social-construction/index.md) - Covers the course's five overarching themes — race, ethnicity, nationality, gender, sexuality — plus intersectionality and how identity categories are socially constructed.
4. [Case Study: Manifest Destiny](04-case-study-manifest-destiny/index.md) - Applies the course's analytical tools to a single historical case — westward expansion, Indigenous displacement, and the frontier myth — as a model for the rest of the course.
5. [Government Representation and Systemic Bias](05-government-representation-and-systemic-bias/index.md) - Examines systemic racism and sexism in policy, gerrymandering, voter suppression, and tribal sovereignty in government relations.
6. [Economic Exclusion](06-economic-exclusion/index.md) - Investigates redlining, embargoes, college admissions bias, and their compounding economic consequences.
7. [The Justice System and the Construction of Criminality](07-justice-system-and-criminality/index.md) - Analyzes how criminality is defined, codes and laws are selectively enforced, and prejudice appears in sentencing and policing.
8. [Representation in the Media](08-representation-in-the-media/index.md) - Studies colorism, beauty standards, headline framing, and media literacy as tools for recognizing bias in representation.
9. [Indigenous Sovereignty and Tribal Relations](09-indigenous-sovereignty-and-tribal-relations/index.md) - Deepens treaty rights, boarding-school history, and contemporary Indigenous voice, fulfilling the Minnesota standards on Tribal Nations.
10. [Migration, Colonialism, and Global Perspectives](10-migration-colonialism-and-global-perspectives/index.md) - Widens the lens to forced migration, diaspora, colonialism, and global patterns of displacement and nationalism.
11. [Civil Rights and Liberation Movements](11-civil-rights-and-liberation-movements/index.md) - Surveys civil rights, suffrage, labor, disability, and LGBTQ+ liberation movements and the strategies that drove legislative change.
12. [Religion and Cultural Diffusion](12-religion-and-cultural-diffusion/index.md) - Traces how world religions and Indigenous spiritual traditions develop and change across social and political contexts.
13. [Language and Power](13-language-and-power/index.md) - Examines how language has been suppressed, revitalized, and used to assert identity and power.
14. [Research Methods and the Capstone Project](14-research-methods-and-capstone-project/index.md) - Equips students with archival research, oral history, and ethical-representation skills to complete the course's capstone: reconstructing an absent narrative of their own.

## How to Use This Textbook

Chapters are ordered so that every concept appears after its prerequisites — work
through them in order on a first pass. The learning graph's dependency structure
means later chapters (Civil Rights and Liberation Movements, the capstone Research
Methods chapter) draw on vocabulary and case studies from every earlier chapter, so
skipping ahead will leave gaps.

---

**Note:** Each chapter includes a list of concepts covered. Make sure to complete prerequisites before moving to advanced chapters.
