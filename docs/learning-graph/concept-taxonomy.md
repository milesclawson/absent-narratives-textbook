# Concept Taxonomy

14 categories organize the 200 concepts in [concept-list.md](./concept-list.md). No category exceeds 9% of the total (well under the 30% ceiling), so no single theme dominates the learning graph.

| Category | TaxonomyID | Count | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| Foundational Historical Thinking Skills | FOUND | 18 | Historiography, source analysis, bias, narrative construction — the analytical toolkit used throughout the course |
| Core Vocabulary & Key Terms | VOCAB | 12 | Shared terminology (oppression, privilege, discrimination, representation) used across every unit |
| Identity & Social Construction | IDENT | 16 | Race, ethnicity, nationality, gender, sexuality, and how identity categories are socially constructed and intersect |
| Manifest Destiny Case Study | MANDEST | 10 | The Unit 1 case study on westward expansion and the narratives it obscured |
| Government Representation & Systemic Bias | GOV | 18 | Unit 2: systemic racism/sexism in policy, gerrymandering, voter suppression, tribal sovereignty |
| Economic Exclusion | ECON | 15 | Unit 3: embargoes, redlining, college admissions bias, and their economic consequences |
| Justice System & Criminality | JUST | 16 | Unit 4: the construction of criminality, codes and laws, sentencing disparities, prejudice statistics |
| Media Representation | MEDIA | 14 | Unit 5: colorism, beauty standards, headline bias, and media literacy |
| Indigenous Sovereignty & Tribal Relations | INDIG | 14 | Tribal sovereignty, treaty rights, boarding schools, and contemporary Indigenous voice in Minnesota and beyond |
| Migration, Colonialism & Global Perspectives | MIGR | 15 | Forced migration, diaspora, colonialism, imperialism, and global patterns of displacement |
| Civil Rights & Liberation Movements | CIVIL | 18 | Civil rights, suffrage, labor, disability, and LGBTQ+ liberation movements and their strategies |
| Religion & Cultural Diffusion | RELIG | 10 | World religions and Indigenous spiritual traditions as they develop and change across social/political contexts |
| Language & Power | LANG | 8 | How language has been used to suppress, revitalize, and assert identity |
| Research Methods & Capstone Skills | RESEARCH | 16 | Archival research, oral history, ethics of representation, and the culminating capstone project |

## Design Notes

- **FOUND** and **VOCAB** together form the shared analytical vocabulary
  every later unit assumes (source bias, systemic oppression, power
  structure). Nearly every concept elsewhere in the graph traces back to one
  of these.
- **IDENT** groups the course's five named overarching themes — race,
  ethnicity, nationality, gender, sexuality — plus intersectionality and the
  social-construction concepts that connect them to every subject-matter
  unit.
- **GOV, ECON, JUST, MEDIA** map directly onto the four subject-matter units
  from the original course proposal (Units 2–5); **MANDEST** captures Unit
  1's case study as its own compact category rather than folding it into
  FOUND.
- **INDIG** is broken out from GOV because the Minnesota state standards
  (9.1.6.2, 9.3.15.x, 9.5.24–25) give tribal sovereignty and Indigenous
  history enough distinct weight to warrant its own category rather than
  being a handful of concepts inside Government Representation.
- **RESEARCH** captures the culminating "Create" level of the course's
  Bloom's Taxonomy outcomes — the skills students need to research and
  reconstruct an absent narrative of their own choosing.
